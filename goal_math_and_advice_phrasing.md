# Goal Math and Advice Phrasing

Applies to: sizing a saving, linking it to an active goal with correct arithmetic, and writing the final one-to-two-sentence advice.

## Converting Between Weekly, Monthly and Yearly Amounts
Use these approximations and keep the numbers rounded and friendly.
- Weekly to monthly: multiply by about 4.33. Weekly to yearly: multiply by 52.
- Monthly to yearly: multiply by 12.
- Weekly examples: $5 is about $22 a month or $260 a year. $10 is about $43 a month or $520 a year. $15 is about $65 a month or $780 a year. $20 is about $87 a month or $1,040 a year. $25 is about $108 a month or $1,300 a year. $30 is about $130 a month or $1,560 a year. $40 is about $173 a month or $2,080 a year.
- Monthly examples: $10 a month is $120 a year, $20 is $240, $30 is $360, and $50 is $600.
State one primary figure per suggestion, with one matching timeframe.

## Sizing a Reduction Realistically
For habit spending such as dining or shopping, aim to trim roughly 20 to 50 percent of the user's observed spend in that category. Choose the low end when the user has rejected earlier advice, when the spend is already modest, or when the move takes effort. For a subscription, pausing or cancelling removes the whole charge. A suggested saving must never exceed what the data shows the user actually spends in that category over the same period.

## Linking the Saving to a Goal Without Inventing Numbers
Name the goal exactly as it appears in the active goals. Show the effect with a simple cumulative figure, such as "adds about $130 a month toward [Goal Name]" or "puts about $780 over the next year toward [Goal Name]".
If both a target and the amount already saved are given, the time to reach the goal is the remaining amount divided by the monthly saving. For example, $600 remaining at $65 a month is about 9 months.
Do not invent target dates, progress figures, or interest. Savings add up linearly. Avoid the word "compounds" unless the data states an interest rate.

## Sentence Structure for the Final Advice
Write one or two sentences in a single plain-text paragraph. The first word must be an action verb such as Trim, Cut, Pause, Cancel, Swap, Cap, Skip, or Review. Name the specific merchant or category, give the amount and timeframe naturally, and end with the active goal it supports. Do not add greetings, praise, disclaimers, markdown, lists, or quotation marks.

## Good Patterns (Illustrative Only)
Never copy these names, amounts, or goals. Always use the user's own data.
- Trim your delivery orders from four a week to two, which frees up about $30 a week and adds roughly $130 a month toward [Goal Name].
- Pause your [Streaming Service] for the next two months to keep its $[amount] monthly charge and put it toward [Goal Name].
- Cap your rideshare spending at $[amount] a week, which would add about $[amount] a month toward [Goal Name].
- Review the two [Merchant] charges that landed within days of each other, since a duplicate refund of $[amount] would go straight toward [Goal Name].

## Patterns to Avoid
- Starting with a greeting or praise ("Great job", "Here is some advice").
- Writing three or more sentences, or adding a closing note or disclaimer.
- Using markdown, bullets, or quotation marks.
- Vague wording such as "spend less on dining" with no amount.
- Naming an amount larger than the user's real spend in that category.
- Targeting rent, housing, insurance, healthcare, or the basic grocery shop.
- Returning to a merchant or category the user rejected or said they value.
- Claiming multiple plans from one merchant, or claiming a service is unused.
- Mentioning a goal that is not in the active goals.

## Tone
Be direct, specific, and encouraging without lecturing. Frame advice as a swap or a trim, not a loss. Do not use guilt, absolute words like "never" or "stop", or promises of guaranteed results. Do not recommend investment products or financial products the data does not mention.
