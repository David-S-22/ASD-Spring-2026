**C. The date engine, in detail.** The hard part; design it first. Status
(paid / due / overdue), per-month estimates and the 180-day timeline are all
computed in ordinary Python from cadence, `next_billing_date` and payment
history — the AI is never asked for arithmetic or date maths. A pure,
dependency-free module that projects a bill over a window (weekly,
fortnightly, monthly); rolls a month into **usual** versus **extra** (a
subscription starting or ending, the third fortnight landing in a month);
derives status from `next_billing_date` and `payments`; and handles
month-end rollover with plain `date` arithmetic. It takes an injectable
demo clock — never `date.today()` inside business logic. Give me function
signatures, edge cases, and test inputs for: the three-fortnight month,
month-end rollover, a subscription starting mid-window, one ending
mid-window, and an overdue bill.

**D. Schema and seed data.** Tables as registered: `bills` (name, merchant,
amount, cadence, next_billing_date, type, payment_method, status),
`payments`, `disputes`, `dispute_drafts` (versioned), and a proposed
`chat_messages` — flag it, do not assume it. **≥10 rows in every table** is
a marking requirement; seed the awkward cases the feature exists for. No
real merchant, bank or account details.

**E. Endpoints.** CRUD over bills, payments and disputes, plus
`GET /timeline?days=30..180` (occurrences tagged actual or predicted),
`GET /calendar/<month>` (the usual/extra breakdown), a read endpoint other
features call for upcoming and predicted bills, and
`POST /disputes/<id>/regenerate`.

**F. The two AI calls, and only two.** Both Frontend → Backend → Ollama →
LLM; no tools, no retrieval.
1. **Draft a dispute** (`llama3.1:8b`): the bill and its payment history go
   in; schema-checked JSON comes back — letter text, a checklist of safe
   steps, an escalation path. If `payment_method` is recorded the steps
   adapt: cancelling in an app versus asking the merchant or bank to remove
   a direct-debit authority. Regenerate feeds my edits back and stores a
   new version.
2. **Chat** (`qwen2.5:0.5b`): a plain-English request becomes exactly one
   structured operation `{op, entity, fields}`. Plan for "I cancelled
   Spotify from September, remove the future payments", "What do my bills
   add up to?", and "Which subscriptions am I barely using?".
For both: the JSON schema and the failure path — validate, retry once, fall
back to a safe default and tell the user. Unvalidated model output never
reaches the database.
