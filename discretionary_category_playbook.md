# Discretionary Spending Category Playbook

Applies to: choosing a realistic, specific saving move once a discretionary category has been picked. Each section works on its own. Always use the user's actual merchants and dollar amounts, and never apply a move the user's feedback has ruled out.

## Eating Out, Takeout and Food Delivery
Signals: frequent restaurant, takeout, or delivery-app charges; delivery and service fees added to orders.
Moves: reduce the number of orders per week (for example, four down to two or three), set a weekly dining allowance, switch some delivery orders to pickup to avoid delivery and service fees, or pack lunch on two workdays.
Typical saving: $15 to $40 a week, usually about 20 to 50 percent of observed weekly dining spend.
Pitfalls: do not suggest eliminating all eating out. Base the figure on the user's real average order size.

## Coffee and Cafe Spending
Signals: several small charges per week at cafes or coffee shops, especially on weekdays.
Moves: make coffee at home on two or three days, or keep one cafe visit as a planned treat each week.
Typical saving: $10 to $25 a week.
Pitfalls: coffee is often a valued ritual, so frame the move as trimming a few visits and never as giving it up. If the user has rejected a coffee suggestion before, avoid the whole eating-out family.

## Digital Subscriptions: Streaming, Music, Apps, Software and Cloud Storage
Signals: recurring charges from the same merchant at monthly or yearly intervals; several services of the same type, such as multiple video streaming services.
Moves: pause or cancel one overlapping service, rotate streaming services month by month, or move from a monthly to an annual plan only when the service is clearly kept long term.
Typical saving: the full charge of the paused or cancelled service, usually $8 to $30 a month.
Pitfalls: transaction data does not show how much a service is used, so never claim it is unused. Say "pause" or "rotate" instead. Do not claim the user has several plans from one merchant unless the data clearly shows it. Two charges from one merchant within a few days point to a duplicate or billing anomaly to review.

## Fitness and Wellness Memberships
Signals: monthly gym, studio, or fitness app charges.
Moves: ask for a membership freeze or pause, move to an off-peak or lower tier, or drop a second fitness app that overlaps with another.
Typical saving: $10 to $40 a month.
Pitfalls: users often value fitness spending highly and reject these suggestions. Make it a lower-priority category unless the data shows overlapping memberships. Never suggest cutting healthcare or medical spending.

## Entertainment, Outings and Events
Signals: cinema, event tickets, venue entries, bars, and in-game or app purchases.
Moves: set a monthly outings allowance, replace one paid outing per month with a free one, or limit in-app purchases to a monthly cap.
Typical saving: $20 to $60 a month.
Pitfalls: separate regular patterns from one-off events, and never build a cap around a single ticket purchase.

## Rideshare and Optional Transport
Signals: repeated rideshare, taxi, or e-scooter charges; paid parking that is not part of a daily commute.
Moves: swap two rides a week for public transit or walking, or set a weekly rideshare cap.
Typical saving: $10 to $30 a week.
Pitfalls: commuting passes, fuel for work travel, and public transit fares used for essential travel are not optional. Do not target them.

## Retail and Impulse Shopping
Signals: many small or irregular purchases from online marketplaces, clothing, electronics, or home goods retailers.
Moves: set a monthly shopping allowance, use a 48-hour wait before non-essential purchases, unsubscribe from promotional emails, or remove saved card details from shopping sites.
Typical saving: $25 to $80 a month.
Pitfalls: do not treat a single large one-off purchase (such as an appliance replacement) as a habit.

## Grocery Extras: The Discretionary Slice Only
Essential grocery spending is never a target. Only the optional slice is: name-brand versus house-brand swaps, convenience-store or snack purchases, and premium specialty items.
Moves: choose house brands for a few regular items, or shift small convenience-store purchases into the weekly shop.
Typical saving: $5 to $20 a week.
Pitfalls: do not tell the user to spend less on groceries overall.

## Avoidable Fees
Signals: ATM fees, late payment fees, overdraft fees, or foreign transaction fees.
Moves: use fee-free ATMs, set payment reminders or autopay for the minimum, or use a card without foreign transaction fees.
Typical saving: the exact fee amount multiplied by how often it appears in the data.
Pitfalls: these moves cost the user nothing in lifestyle, so they are well received, but only suggest them when fee charges actually appear in the data.

## Gray Zone: Phone, Internet and Plan Tiers
Plans such as mobile or internet tiers are fixed bills with an optional element. The savings guide allows reviewing plan tiers, so suggest this only when no pure discretionary option remains and the user has not objected. Never target rent, mortgage, utilities tied to housing, insurance, or healthcare.
