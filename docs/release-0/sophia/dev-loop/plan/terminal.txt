### C. The date engine, in detail
This is the hard part of my feature and I want it designed before anything
else exists. Status (paid / due / overdue), the per-month estimates and the
180-day timeline are all computed in **ordinary Python** from each bill's
cadence, `next_billing_date` and payment history. The AI is never asked for
arithmetic or date maths.

Design a pure, dependency-free module that can:
- project a bill forward over a window and return its occurrence dates
  (cadence is weekly, fortnightly or monthly)
- roll a month up into **usual** (the steady recurring load) versus **extra**
  (a subscription starting or ending, and the third fortnight when one lands
  in the month), so the calendar can say: "September needs up to $436 — your
  usual weekly and fortnightly bills come to $379–415, plus Netflix starts
  ($21) and an extra Anytime Fitness payment because three fortnights land in
  September"
- derive status from `next_billing_date` and the `payments` table
- handle month-end rollover (a monthly bill dated the 31st) using plain
  `date` arithmetic, no timezones

It must take an injectable **demo clock** — a single "today" passed in —
never `date.today()` inside business logic, so the tests are stable and the
showcase demo is reproducible.

Give me the function signatures, the edge cases you would test, and the
specific test inputs for: the three-fortnight month, month-end rollover, a
subscription starting mid-window, one ending mid-window, and an overdue bill.
