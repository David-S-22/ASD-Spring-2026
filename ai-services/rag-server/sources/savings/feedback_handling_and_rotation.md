# Handling User Feedback and Rotating Savings Suggestions

Applies to: deciding which spending area to target next after earlier suggestions were accepted, rejected, or commented on by the user.

## Rejected Suggestions: Never Re-Target the Same Merchant or Category
When a user rejects a suggestion, the merchant and the spending category it targeted are closed for all future suggestions, whatever reason was given. Pivot to a different spending family (see "Spending Families"), not just a different merchant in the same family. A rejection is a signal about the user's priorities. It is not an invitation to offer a smaller number on the same item. The rejection reason should change how the next suggestion is pitched (size, effort, framing), never which category it returns to.

## Spending Families: Avoiding Near-Duplicate Suggestions
Group discretionary spending into families. If a user rejects a suggestion or says they value something in a family, avoid the whole family unless their feedback names only one specific merchant.
- Eating out: restaurants, takeout, food delivery apps, cafes, coffee, bars
- Digital subscriptions: streaming video and music, apps, software, cloud storage, news, gaming subscriptions
- Fitness and wellness: gym memberships, classes, fitness apps, studio passes
- Entertainment and outings: cinema, events, venues, hobbies, in-game purchases
- Optional transport: rideshare, taxis, e-scooters, optional parking
- Retail and impulse shopping: clothing, electronics, home goods, online marketplaces, hobby supplies
- Grocery extras: premium brands, convenience-store and snack purchases (never the basic grocery total)
- Avoidable fees: ATM fees, late fees, overdraft fees, foreign transaction fees

## Interpreting Rejection Reasons to Shape the Next Suggestion
Use the stated reason to decide the tone, size, and effort of the next suggestion in a different family.
- "I value this" or "this is important to me": mark that merchant and family as protected. Next, prefer convenience or habit spending the user is less likely to feel attached to.
- "Too much", "too aggressive", or "unrealistic": make the next suggestion smaller (low end of the typical range) and easier to carry out.
- "Too much effort" or "inconvenient": prefer low-friction moves such as pausing a subscription or setting a cap over habit changes like meal prepping.
- "Already doing this" or "already cancelled": treat that spend as handled. Do not suggest it again, even if older transactions still show it.
- "Wrong" or "not accurate" (shared account, reimbursed, business expense): do not treat that merchant's spending as discretionary. Be cautious with similar-looking transactions.
- "Bad timing" (travel, holiday, event, life change): pick an area unaffected by the situation and do not circle back soon.
- "Doesn't help my goal" or "wrong goal": link the next suggestion to a different active goal if one exists, and favour a larger or more visible saving.
- No reason given: treat it as protecting that family and keep the next suggestion moderate in size.

## Accepted Suggestions: What They Tell You
An accepted suggestion means that spend has been addressed. Do not stack a second reduction on the same merchant or category. The accepted amount shows what the user finds comfortable, so the next suggestion can be a similar size, or up to about 1.5 times larger, in a different family. Do not assume the saving actually happened unless later transactions show it. Do not repeat accepted advice as a reminder.

## Stated Preferences Count as Standing Instructions
Statements such as "I love my gym", "coffee is my one treat", or "I need my streaming" work like rejections that last for the whole conversation history. Protect the named merchant and its family in every future suggestion. If a preference conflicts with a retrieved guideline, the preference wins.

## Choosing the Next Spending Area (Rotation Steps)
1. List every family and merchant already touched by an accepted, rejected, or recent suggestion, plus any protected by stated preferences.
2. From the remaining families, list those with meaningful spend in the user's data (roughly at least $5 a week or $20 a month).
3. Prefer the family with the largest realistic saving that also fits the rejection and acceptance signals above.
4. Break ties by lowest effort for the user.
5. Avoid two consecutive suggestions from the same family, even when both were accepted.

## When No Fresh Spending Area Remains
If every family with meaningful spend has already been accepted, rejected, or protected, there is no grounded fresh suggestion left. Do not recycle a rejected category and do not invent spending. Treat the situation as insufficient context for a new suggestion.
