# Interpreting Transaction Data Correctly

Applies to: reading transaction lists and merchant spending summaries before turning them into savings advice. Use only what the data shows.

## Which Transactions Count as Discretionary Spending
Count spending on wants and optional habits: eating out, coffee, subscriptions, entertainment, optional transport, retail and impulse shopping, grocery extras, and avoidable fees.
Exclude these entirely: rent and mortgage, utilities tied to housing, insurance, healthcare, debt minimum payments, and the basic weekly grocery shop.
Also exclude money movements that are not spending: income, transfers between the user's own accounts, savings contributions, and credit card or loan repayments.

## Classifying a Merchant When No Category Is Given
Classify by what the merchant mainly sells. Use the merchant name and the typical charge size together. Small repeated charges at food-and-drink merchants are usually coffee or takeout. A fixed amount repeating about every month is usually a subscription. If a merchant cannot be classified confidently, leave it out rather than guess.

## Recognising Recurring Charges and Billing Cadence
A recurring subscription shows up as the same merchant, a similar or identical amount, and charges roughly 28 to 31 days apart (monthly) or about 365 days apart (annual). Subscriptions bill monthly or annually, never weekly. When describing a subscription saving, use a monthly figure, and use an annual figure only when the cadence is clear from the data. If only one charge is visible, describe it as "your $X charge" and do not assert a yearly total.

## Duplicate and Anomalous Charges
Two charges from the same merchant within a few days, in the same or a similar amount, indicate a possible duplicate charge or billing error to review. They do not indicate two separate plans or accounts. Different amounts from one merchant in a month could be add-ons or price changes, and must not be described as multiple plans unless the data explicitly shows it.

## Refunds, Reversals and Pending Items
Subtract refunds and reversals from the matching merchant's total. Do not count a charge that was reversed. If a charge is marked pending, treat it as tentative and avoid basing advice on it alone.

## Habit Spending Versus One-Off Purchases
A habit is a pattern: several similar charges across multiple weeks. A one-off is a single large or unusual purchase. Build weekly caps and frequency changes around habits only. Do not extrapolate a one-off purchase into a weekly or monthly figure.

## Using the Pre-Calculated Spending Summary
When a pre-calculated merchant summary is provided, treat its totals as authoritative and do not re-add transactions to produce a different number. Use the transaction list for dates, cadence, and frequency. Work out the time span covered by the data from the earliest to the latest transaction date, and only convert totals to weekly or monthly averages over that real span.

## Short Data Windows
If the data covers less than about two weeks, treat weekly averages as rough. Prefer per-order or per-visit framing (for example, "skip two orders at about $18 each"). Do not project yearly totals from a very short window.

## Concrete Thresholds for Sufficient Context
These thresholds make the context requirement in the savings guide and budgeting principles concrete. They refine those statements and do not replace them.
- At least one active goal with a name and a target amount.
- Roughly 10 or more recorded spending transactions covering about two weeks or more.
- At least one discretionary category where a realistic saving of about $5 a week or $20 a month is possible.
A clearly identifiable recurring subscription charge can ground a subscription suggestion even when the history is short. If any requirement is missing, do not speculate or invent figures.

## Never Invent Facts
Do not state usage levels the data cannot show (for example, "you rarely use this"). Do not name merchants, amounts, goals, or dates that are not in the provided data. Do not claim the user has more than one plan or account with a merchant unless the data shows it.
