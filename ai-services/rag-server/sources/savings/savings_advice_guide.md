# Guidelines for Generating Personal Savings Advice

## Core Methodology and Principles
To generate effective, actionable savings advice, personal financial advisors must analyze a user's recent spending patterns, category distributions, recurring commitments, and active financial goals. All advice should empower the user to make realistic, incremental changes to discretionary spending habits rather than sacrificing essential living needs.

## Discretionary Spending Reductions
- Analyze discretionary expenses such as dining out, takeout meals, premium coffee, entertainment, rideshares, and impulse retail shopping.
- Recommend practical reductions rather than complete elimination: propose weekly caps, reducing visit frequency (e.g. dining out twice a week instead of four times), or choosing house brands over name brands.
- Never suggest cutting essential non-discretionary payments such as rent, mortgage, healthcare, insurance, or essential groceries.

## Goal-Oriented Causal Linking
- Every piece of savings advice must clearly link recommended dollar reductions directly to the user's active savings goals.
- Explicitly explain the financial causality: demonstrate how freeing up an amount (e.g. $15 to $30 a week) compounds to reach a specific milestone or target date for an emergency fund, home deposit, or vacation goal.

## Habit Adjustments and Cadence Optimization
- Identify spending cadence: distinguish regular weekly patterns from sporadic splurges.
- Recommend setting spending thresholds or cadence limits (e.g. setting a $40 weekly dining allowance).
- Encourage gradual behavioral shifts (e.g. meal prepping or packing lunch two days a week to trim $20-$30 weekly).

## Subscription Auditing and Fixed Bill Optimization
- Audit recurring subscriptions (streaming services, gym memberships, software apps) to identify unused or redundant memberships.
- Suggest pausing, rotating, or canceling services that overlap or are seldom utilized.
- Recommend reviewing utility rates, negotiating lower plan tiers, or eliminating duplicate services.

## Minimum Context Requirements for Advisors
- Accurate savings advice requires sufficient financial context: active savings goals with defined targets and recorded transaction history.
- If financial data, transaction categories, or baseline context are insufficient or missing, advisors must withhold recommendations until adequate context is available.
