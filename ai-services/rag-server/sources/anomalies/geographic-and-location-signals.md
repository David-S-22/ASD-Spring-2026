# Geographic and location signals

Reference guidance for judging a transaction by where it appears to originate.
Location signals are circumstantial, so weigh them alongside amount, merchant,
and timing rather than on their own.

## Impossible travel
Two charges in locations too far apart to travel between in the elapsed time
suggest a cloned card or compromised credentials. The shorter the gap and the
greater the distance, the stronger the signal.

## Unfamiliar regions
A charge from a country or region the account holder has never transacted in,
especially one known for high card-fraud rates, warrants a closer look when paired
with an unusual amount or merchant.

## Foreign currency and conversion
Unexpected foreign-currency charges, or domestic accounts suddenly billed in an
overseas currency, can indicate a disguised or fraudulent merchant. A single
expected travel charge is normal; a cluster of them without travel history is not.

## Online charges with mismatched geography
For card-not-present purchases, a billing location that conflicts with the
account holder's usual region, or rapidly changing locations across charges, is a
mild red flag that strengthens in combination.

## Guidance
Treat location as supporting evidence. State the specific geographic conflict —
distance, region, or currency — when it contributes to flagging a transaction.
