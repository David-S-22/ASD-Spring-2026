# Spending baselines and deviations

Reference guidance for judging a transaction against the account holder's normal
behaviour. An anomaly is usually a departure from an established baseline, so
compare the current charge to the person's own history before flagging it.

## Establishing a baseline
A baseline is the typical range of a person's spending for a given merchant,
category, or time of month. It is built from recurring bills, regular groceries,
transport, and discretionary habits. Judge a new charge against this range
rather than against absolute thresholds.

## Amount deviations
A charge that is several times larger than the usual amount for that merchant or
category is a deviation worth noting. Small overages are normal; large,
unexplained jumps are not. Weigh the size of the jump against how consistent the
history has been.

## New or first-time merchants
A first charge to a merchant the account holder has never used removes the
baseline entirely. Combined with a large amount or a high-risk category, an
unfamiliar merchant deserves closer review; on its own it is only a weak signal.

## Category drift
Spending that falls outside the person's usual categories — for example a sudden
luxury, gambling, or electronics charge on an account that only ever sees bills
and groceries — is a mild signal, stronger when paired with an unusual amount.

## Frequency changes
A merchant that normally bills monthly suddenly charging several times in a week,
or a dormant payee becoming active again, is a frequency deviation. Repeated
charges in a short window are a stronger signal than a single off-pattern charge.

## Guidance
Prefer evidence of deviation from the person's own baseline over fixed dollar
limits. State which baseline the charge departs from, and by how much, when
explaining why a transaction looks anomalous.
