# Velocity and timing patterns

Reference guidance for judging a transaction by its timing and the pace of
surrounding activity. Velocity signals describe how charges cluster in time.

## Rapid successive charges
Several charges in quick succession, especially to the same or related merchants,
can indicate automated or fraudulent activity. The tighter the cluster, the
stronger the signal.

## Escalating amounts
A small charge followed shortly by a much larger one fits the card-testing
pattern, where a trivial charge validates a card before a big attempt. The
sequence matters more than either charge alone.

## Odd-hour activity
Charges at hours the account holder is normally inactive, or on future-dated
timestamps, are mild signals that strengthen when paired with unfamiliar
merchants or unusual amounts.

## Dormant-then-active bursts
An account or payee that is quiet for a long period then suddenly produces a
burst of activity is worth reviewing, particularly if the burst includes
high-risk categories.

## Guidance
Describe the specific timing pattern — cluster, escalation, odd hour, or burst —
and avoid flagging ordinary, evenly spaced spending as anomalous.
