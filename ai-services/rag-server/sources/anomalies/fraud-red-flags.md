# Transaction fraud red flags

Reference guidance for reviewing whether a single financial transaction looks
suspicious. Each section lists signals that, alone or combined, justify flagging
a transaction for human review.

## Unusually large amounts
Transactions that are large relative to a person's typical spending are a common
fraud signal. Very high values, or amounts near common card/account limits,
warrant a closer look.

## Suspiciously round amounts
Fraudulent or test charges often use round numbers (for example 100.00, 500.00,
1000.00). Legitimate retail purchases usually carry cents and tax, so a large
perfectly round amount is a mild red flag.

## Cash-like and high-risk merchants
Charges to ATMs, cash-advance services, money transfers, gift-card resellers,
cryptocurrency exchanges, and prepaid-card vendors are higher risk because funds
are hard to recover once moved.

## Generic or unfamiliar merchant names
Vague, generic, or unrecognisable merchant descriptors (for example "Global
Services", "Online Purchase", random strings) can indicate a disguised or
fraudulent merchant.

## Unusual timing
Charges at odd hours, on future-dated timestamps, or clustered in rapid
succession can indicate automated or fraudulent activity.

## Combinations
Individually weak signals become stronger together. A large, round charge to an
unfamiliar cash-like merchant at an odd hour is far more suspicious than any one
of those traits alone.
