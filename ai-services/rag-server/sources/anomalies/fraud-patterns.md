# Common card and account fraud patterns

Descriptions of recurring fraud patterns that help explain why a transaction was
flagged. Use these to ground an explanation in a recognised pattern.

## Card testing
Fraudsters validate stolen card numbers with small charges before attempting a
large one. A small unfamiliar charge followed by a much larger charge to the
same or a related merchant fits this pattern.

## Account takeover
After gaining access to an account, an attacker often moves money to a new payee,
a transfer service, or a cash-out channel. Sudden transfers to unfamiliar
recipients are a hallmark.

## Merchant misclassification
Charges where the merchant category does not match the described purchase can
indicate a disguised transaction (for example a "grocery" merchant billing a
large electronics-sized amount).

## Subscription and free-trial traps
Recurring charges from unfamiliar merchants shortly after a free trial, or small
recurring amounts the account holder does not recognise, are worth flagging.

## Duplicate and repeated charges
The same amount charged multiple times in a short window, or identical charges to
the same merchant, can indicate a processing error or fraud.
