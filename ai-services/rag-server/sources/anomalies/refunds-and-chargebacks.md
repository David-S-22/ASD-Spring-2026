# Refunds and chargebacks

Reference guidance for reviewing credits, refunds, and reversals. These move money
back to the account, so the anomaly patterns differ from ordinary purchases.

## Refunds without a matching purchase
A credit from a merchant the account holder never paid, or a refund with no
corresponding original charge, is a strong signal of error or fraud. Look for the
purchase the refund should offset.

## Mismatched refund amounts
A refund larger than the original purchase, or many small refunds that together
exceed what was ever spent, can indicate manipulation. Compare the refund total
against the related purchases.

## Rapid purchase-then-refund cycles
Quick cycles of charge and reversal to the same merchant can be used to test
cards or launder funds. Repeated tight cycles are more suspicious than a single
return.

## Chargeback-prone merchants
Frequent disputes or reversals involving the same merchant can indicate an
unreliable or fraudulent seller. Treat a pattern of reversals as a stronger
signal than any one refund.

## Guidance
Judge credits against the purchases they relate to. When flagging a refund, state
the missing or mismatched original charge that makes it look anomalous.
